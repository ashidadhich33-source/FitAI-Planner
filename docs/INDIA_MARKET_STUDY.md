# FitAI Planner — Ocean-Deep India Market & Product Study (2026)

_Last updated: 16 May 2026_

## 1. Executive Summary

FitAI Planner should be positioned as an **India-first AI fitness planning platform** that converts a user's basic profile into a practical calorie target, macro split, Indian diet plan, workout plan, and monthly progression roadmap.

The strongest opportunity is not “AI PDF generation.” The stronger business is a **recurring AI fitness operating system for Indian beginners and intermediate gym-goers** who are confused about calories, protein, bulking/cutting, workouts, and Indian food choices.

### Why this can win in India

1. **Large and growing market:** Deloitte India and the Health & Fitness Association estimate India’s fitness market will grow from about **₹16,200 crore in 2024 to ₹37,700 crore by 2030**, implying roughly **15% CAGR**.[^deloitte-fitness]
2. **Digital distribution tailwind:** IAMAI-Kantar reporting indicates India is crossing **900M+ internet users**, with rural and regional-language usage becoming a major growth driver.[^iamai-900m]
3. **Fitness app demand:** IMARC estimates India’s fitness app market at **US$521.06M in 2025**, projected to reach **US$2.91B by 2034**.[^imarc-fitness-app]
4. **Clear India-specific gap:** Most global fitness plans are not built around roti, dal, paneer, poha, rajma, chana, khichdi, curd, dosa, idli, regional food habits, vegetarian protein constraints, and Indian monthly food budgets.
5. **Recurring need:** Users need updated plans after weight changes, plateaus, travel, festivals, exams, night shifts, budget changes, and goal switches.

### Recommended wedge

Start with a focused promise:

> “Get a personalized Indian diet + workout plan in 2 minutes based on your goal, budget, food preference, and gym/home setup.”

Then expand into:

- AI chat coach
- progress tracking
- adaptive calories/macros
- transformation roadmap
- habit streaks
- trainer dashboard
- WhatsApp-based retention
- regional-language plans

## 2. Target Customer Segments in India

### Segment A — Beginner gym joiners

**Profile:** 16–30 years old, first 0–12 months in gym, follows Instagram fitness content, confused about diet and workout structure.

**Pain points:**

- Does not know calorie target or protein requirement.
- Copies random influencer workouts.
- Eats Indian home food but follows foreign diet videos.
- Wants visible transformation quickly.
- Often cannot afford personal coaching.

**Best hook:**

> “Stop guessing your diet. Get your Indian fat-loss or muscle-gain plan in 2 minutes.”

### Segment B — College students and hostel users

**Profile:** Budget-sensitive, limited cooking control, relies on mess food, canteen food, eggs, milk, peanut butter, bananas, whey if affordable.

**Pain points:**

- Low budget.
- Mess food uncertainty.
- Irregular sleep and classes.
- Wants cheap protein options.

**Best hook:**

> “₹150/day hostel-friendly muscle gain diet.”

### Segment C — Working professionals

**Profile:** 22–40 years old, sedentary jobs, weight gain, stress, food delivery, limited workout time.

**Pain points:**

- Belly fat.
- Poor consistency.
- Eating out.
- Needs 30–45 minute workouts.
- Wants practical meal swaps, not strict bodybuilding diet.

**Best hook:**

> “Fat-loss plan that works with office lunch, chai, roti, rice, and Indian dinners.”

### Segment D — Vegetarian fitness users

**Profile:** Large Indian audience that struggles with protein.

**Pain points:**

- Paneer is calorie-dense.
- Dal is not as protein-dense as people think.
- Soya, curd, milk, whey, tofu, chana, rajma need careful planning.
- Family food constraints.

**Best hook:**

> “Vegetarian high-protein Indian diet without boring meals.”

### Segment E — Tier-2/Tier-3 aspirational users

**Profile:** Consumes Hindi/regional reels, budget-conscious, may use local gym, needs simpler UI and local language.

**Pain points:**

- English-heavy apps feel intimidating.
- Global food databases feel irrelevant.
- Personal trainers may be unaffordable or inconsistent.

**Best hook:**

> “Hindi/Indian food fitness coach for your exact budget and goal.”

## 3. Core Problem Statement

Indian users do not fail only because of motivation. They fail because they lack a simple, personalized system that answers:

- Should I bulk, cut, recomp, or maintain?
- How many calories should I eat?
- How much protein do I need?
- What should I eat if my home food is roti, rice, dal, sabzi, curd, paneer, eggs, chicken, poha, upma, idli, dosa, rajma, chana, and khichdi?
- How do I train at home vs gym?
- How do I progress every week?
- What should I change after a plateau?
- Which supplements are useful, optional, or unnecessary?

FitAI Planner should become the “decision engine” for these questions.

## 8. Formula and Recommendation Engine

### 8.1 BMI

```text
BMI = weight_kg / (height_m * height_m)
```

BMI is only a screening indicator and should not be treated as a complete fitness diagnosis.

### 8.2 BMR

Use Mifflin-St Jeor as a default formula:

```text
Male BMR   = 10 × weight_kg + 6.25 × height_cm - 5 × age + 5
Female BMR = 10 × weight_kg + 6.25 × height_cm - 5 × age - 161
```

For non-binary/other gender selection, the app should either ask for calculation preference or provide a neutral estimate range.

### 8.3 TDEE

```text
TDEE = BMR × activity_factor
```

Suggested activity factors:

| Activity level | Factor |
|---|---:|
| Sedentary | 1.2 |
| Lightly active | 1.375 |
| Moderately active | 1.55 |
| Very active | 1.725 |

### 8.4 Goal calories

| Goal | Suggested adjustment |
|---|---:|
| Fat loss | TDEE - 15% to 25% |
| Lean bulk | TDEE + 5% to 10% |
| Muscle gain | TDEE + 10% to 15% |
| Maintenance/recomp | TDEE ± 0% to 5% |

### 8.5 Protein targets

Use body-weight-based ranges and explain them simply:

| User type | Protein target |
|---|---:|
| Beginner fat loss | 1.6–2.0 g/kg/day |
| Lean bulk/muscle gain | 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day |
| Overweight beginner | Use target/adjusted body weight to avoid unrealistic targets |
| Sedentary general wellness | Lower range, with food-first guidance |

India-specific note: vegetarian users may need deliberate protein planning because many Indian vegetarian staples are carbohydrate-heavy.

## 9. Indian Food AI — Core Feature Design

### 9.1 Food database priorities

Build the database around real Indian foods before global foods:

- Grains: roti, rice, poha, upma, idli, dosa, oats, dalia.
- Pulses: dal, rajma, chana, sprouts, moong, masoor.
- Dairy: curd, milk, paneer, chaas, Greek yogurt/hung curd.
- Protein: eggs, chicken, fish, soya chunks, tofu, whey.
- Fats: ghee, oil, peanuts, peanut butter, nuts.
- Vegetables: sabzi templates by oil quantity.
- Snacks: fruit, makhana, roasted chana, peanuts, sandwich, sprouts chaat.
- Eating out: biryani, dosa, thali, pav bhaji, chole bhature, rolls, momos.

### 9.2 Budget examples

#### ₹150/day vegetarian plan concept

- Breakfast: poha/upma + curd or milk.
- Lunch: rice/roti + dal + seasonal sabzi.
- Snack: roasted chana/banana/peanuts.
- Dinner: roti + soya/paneer-limited/tofu if budget allows + salad.

#### ₹300/day high-protein vegetarian plan concept

- Breakfast: oats + milk + peanut butter.
- Lunch: roti/rice + dal + curd + sabzi.
- Snack: whey or hung curd + fruit.
- Dinner: soya/tofu/paneer rotation + roti + salad.

#### Non-veg plan concept

- Breakfast: eggs + roti/oats/poha.
- Lunch: rice/roti + chicken/fish/egg curry + salad.
- Snack: fruit + curd/whey.
- Dinner: dal + eggs/chicken/fish + vegetables.

### 9.3 Smart meal swaps

The AI should always provide swaps, because Indian users rarely follow fixed meal plans perfectly.

Examples:

- 100g paneer → eggs/tofu/soya/chicken depending on preference.
- Rice lunch → roti lunch with equivalent calories.
- Whey unaffordable → milk + curd + soya + eggs if acceptable.
- Missed breakfast → larger lunch protein + evening snack.
- Festival meal → reduce fats/carbs in earlier/later meals, do not crash diet.

## 10. Workout Plan Logic

### 10.1 Beginner gym plan

For most beginners, avoid complex bodybuilding splits. Use full-body or upper/lower first.

| Days/week | Recommended split |
|---:|---|
| 3 | Full body A/B |
| 4 | Upper/lower |
| 5 | Upper/lower + weak point/full body |
| 6 | Push/pull/legs |

### 10.2 Exercise output format

Every exercise should include:

- Sets
- Reps
- Rest time
- RPE or effort level
- Technique cue
- Progression rule
- Replacement exercise

Example:

| Exercise | Sets | Reps | Rest | Note |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Bench press | 3 | 6–10 | 2 min | Add weight after hitting 10 reps in all sets |
| Incline dumbbell press | 3 | 8–12 | 90 sec | Controlled lowering |
| Shoulder press | 3 | 8–12 | 90 sec | Avoid back arch |
| Tricep pushdown | 3 | 12–15 | 60 sec | Full lockout |

### 10.3 Home workout plan

Home plans should not be treated as inferior. They should include:

- Push-up progressions.
- Squat/lunge variations.
- Hip hinge options.
- Backpack rows.
- Resistance band options.
- Dumbbell substitutions.
- Core training.

## 11. AI Architecture Strategy

The phrase “create own inbuilt AI” should be translated into a practical phased architecture.

### Phase 1 — Rules + LLM generation

Use deterministic formulas for BMI/BMR/TDEE/macros and use an LLM only to turn structured data into a friendly plan.

Why:

- Safer.
- Faster to launch.
- Less hallucination risk.
- Easier to validate outputs.

### Phase 2 — Retrieval-augmented Indian fitness knowledge base

Build an internal knowledge base containing:

- Indian food nutrition data.
- Diet templates.
- Workout templates.
- Safety policies.
- Supplement guidance.
- Regional food swaps.
- Budget rules.

The AI should retrieve from this controlled knowledge base before generating.

### Phase 3 — Personalization memory

Store user history:

- Weight logs.
- Diet adherence.
- Workout completion.
- Strength progress.
- Hunger and energy feedback.
- Missed meals.

Use that data to update calories, macros, meals, and workouts.

### Phase 4 — Proprietary recommendation model

Only after enough anonymized user data exists, train or fine-tune internal models for:

- Meal preference prediction.
- Drop-off risk.
- Plateau detection.
- Budget-friendly food recommendations.
- Workout adherence prediction.

### Important AI safety rule

The app should never generate medical treatment plans. For diabetes, hypertension, pregnancy, eating disorder history, kidney disease, heart disease, thyroid medication, PCOS complications, or injury rehab, the product should show a referral/consultation warning.

- Create upsell to subscription.

If selling ₹199/month Basic:

- Razorpay-like payment cost: roughly 2% + GST on fee.
- AI generation cost: must be controlled with templates, caching, and compact prompts.
- Gross margin can be high if outputs are template-assisted.

## 16. Trust, Compliance, and Safety

### 16.1 Must-have disclaimers

- Not medical advice.
- Not a replacement for doctor/dietician/trainer.
- Consult a professional for medical conditions.
- Stop exercises causing pain.
- Avoid extreme diets.

### 16.2 Sensitive user groups

Require warning or professional referral for:

- Under 18 users.
- Pregnant/lactating users.
- Diabetes.
- Kidney disease.
- Heart disease.
- Eating disorder history.
- Severe obesity.
- Hypertension.
- Major injuries.

### 16.3 Supplement policy

Avoid exaggerated supplement claims.

Allowed framing:

- “Optional.”
- “Convenience.”
- “Discuss with a professional if you have medical conditions.”

Avoid:

- Guaranteed fat loss.
- Steroid-like claims.
- Disease treatment claims.
- Aggressive fat burner recommendations.

### 16.4 Data privacy

Fitness data can be sensitive. Minimum requirements:

- Clear privacy policy.
- Data deletion request flow.
- Secure auth.
- Encrypted secrets.
- No public sharing without consent.
- Separate analytics from personally identifiable information where possible.

## 21. Prompting Strategy

Use structured prompts, not free-form prompts.

### System behavior

- Use calculated calories/macros exactly.
- Do not invent medical claims.
- Prefer Indian foods.
- Respect budget.
- Respect allergies and diet preference.
- Provide swaps.
- Use simple language.
- Include safety disclaimer.

### AI input

Pass:

- User profile.
- Calculated targets.
- Allowed food list.
- Restricted food list.
- Budget tier.
- Workout equipment.
- Template plan options.

### AI output

Require JSON first, then render to UI/PDF. This makes the output testable and easier to validate.

## 22. Design Principles

1. **Beginner-first language:** Avoid overcomplicated fitness jargon.
2. **India-first meals:** Do not make oats/chicken/broccoli the only default.
3. **Budget realism:** A ₹150/day plan and ₹500/day plan should be visibly different.
4. **No shame:** Use supportive tone, not guilt.
5. **Fast value:** Show useful calculations before payment.
6. **Visual clarity:** Cards, charts, progress bars, meal tables.
7. **Mobile-first:** Most Indian users will arrive from social/mobile.

## 25. Final Recommendation

This idea is strong if it is executed as a **personalized Indian fitness system**, not just a PDF tool.

The winning strategy is:

1. Launch a fast MVP that calculates calories/macros and generates Indian diet/workout plans.
2. Sell a low-ticket ₹99 plan to validate demand.
3. Convert serious users to ₹199–₹499/month subscriptions.
4. Build retention with weekly check-ins and AI chat.
5. Use Indian food, budget planning, Hindi/regional content, and gym partnerships as the moat.

The product should be built with a rules-first engine and AI-assisted personalization. That creates safer, cheaper, more consistent plans than relying on a raw chatbot alone.
